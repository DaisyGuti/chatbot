Grayson Lafrenz Founder / CEO In boardrooms around the world, the AI conversation has moved past "if" and landed squarely on "how." Executives know AI is no longer an experimental tool—it's a transformational force. But despite growing interest, most companies still don't have a centralized, integrated AI strategy that ties directly to ROI. A powerful AI strategy must be: • Designed for today and the next 3–5 years • Aligned with your company's core business drivers • Backed by measurable ROI: revenue growth, margin expansion, cost reduction, and scalable operations • Built for execution—not just theory That's exactly where a world-class AI strategy and integration firm steps in. Here's why hiring the right firm is one of the smartest decisions you can make for your company's future: ‍ 1. Committees Aren't Built to Drive AI Transformation ‍ Most companies attempt to develop their AI roadmap through internal committees. These groups are well-meaning—but under-resourced and overextended. Why committees fall short: • Competing priorities: AI strategy becomes secondary to daily operations • Lack of AI expertise: No AI engineers, product leaders, or data architects on the team • Siloed thinking: Limited exposure to cross-functional and cross-industry applications • Slow execution: Group consensus takes time, often delaying decisions by months Some stats that we think matter: • 85% of AI projects fail due to misalignment, poor planning, or lack of ownership (Gartner) • Only 11% of organizations have scaled AI successfully (McKinsey, 2023) Takeaway: AI is not a part-time initiative. It demands full-time strategy and execution. ‍ 2. You Get a Multidisciplinary Powerhouse, Built Around Your Business ‍ AI isn't one skillset—it's a convergence of many. A top-tier firm like Cadre AI assembles a tailored team around your business needs. Your dedicated team includes: • AI engineers to build and fine-tune models • Data engineers to ensure your infrastructure is AI-ready • Business strategists to align use cases with business objectives • Financial analysts to model ROI and support investment decisions • AI product leaders to prioritize features and iterate solutions • Project managers to drive delivery and accountability Why this matters: • Internal teams typically lack 70–80% of these roles • Multidisciplinary teams reduce false starts and costly missteps • You move faster, with less risk ‍ 3. It's Not Just Tech—It's About the Business Model ‍ The best AI strategies don't start with tools—they start with your economic engine. Key questions an AI firm helps answer: • How does your company generate revenue today? • Where are the hidden inefficiencies in your cost structure? • How can AI expand margins or diversify revenue streams? • What's the current EBITDA contribution—and how can AI improve it? Benefits of business-first AI design: • Aligns initiatives with P&L drivers • Prioritizes investments based on financial impact • Reduces wasted time on "cool tech" that doesn't move the business ‍ 4. AI Strategy is Worthless Without Integration ‍ Coming up with AI ideas is easy. Bringing them to life across people, processes, and systems? That's the hard part. A strong firm manages: • Workflow integration into existing tools and CRMs • Automation hand-offs between AI systems and human teams • Data readiness and ongoing maintenance • Security and compliance checks, especially in regulated industries Stats to note: • 70% of digital transformations fail due to poor integration planning (BCG) • 60% of AI models are never deployed into production (VentureBeat, 2023) ‍ 5. Change Management is Half the Battle ‍ Even the best AI solution fails if people don't use it. Adoption isn't automatic—it must be engineered. Effective firms guide change by: • Communicating the "why" of every AI initiative • Creating training and upskilling plans • Addressing cultural and political friction points • Driving executive and team alignment Why this matters: • Only 34% of employees feel confident using new AI tools (PwC, 2023) • Change-resistant cultures can kill even the best strategy ‍ 6. Speed Matters. So Does Focus. ‍ Internal teams often spend 6–12 months just scoping their AI vision. A firm brings speed and intensity that's otherwise impossible. With a dedicated firm, you get: • Faster strategy sprints (2–4 weeks) • MVPs in-market in 60–90 days • Real feedback loops to iterate with data Why this works: It keeps momentum high, minimizes internal distractions, and ensures faster time to value. ‍ 7. You Need Future-Proofing, Not Just Quick Wins ‍ Many AI initiatives are shortsighted—focused on today's tools, not tomorrow's capabilities. What future-proofing includes: • Staying ahead of evolving model capabilities (e.g., GPT-5, open-source advances) • Planning for data infrastructure needs 2–3 years ahead • Understanding regulatory trends (e.g., EU AI Act, U.S. policy shifts) • Building adaptability into your AI tech stack Result: Your AI strategy stays relevant longer—and becomes a durable advantage. ‍ 8. AI is a Moving Target—You Need Iteration, Not Just Implementation ‍ Static plans don't survive contact with real users or data. The best firms build feedback loops into the process. Continuous improvement means: • Regular usage tracking and performance reporting • Iterative product cycles based on real business feedback • Ongoing re-training of models for accuracy and relevance • Identifying second- and third-wave use cases from initial wins Bottom line: The strategy evolves as your business grows and the tech matures. ‍ 9. Better Alignment Across the Executive Team and the Greater Employee Base ‍ Strategy created externally often brings stronger alignment internally. A third-party firm can facilitate consensus and accelerate decisions by bringing their own use cases and expertise, talking with key c-level stakeholders and also working hands-on with the actual people in the company and greater team to give them a voice and make it easy for those employees to share their ideas, fears, and candid feedback. What this looks like: • Workshops that bring finance, ops, product, and tech together • External facilitators that ask tough questions and foster shared vision • Documentation that clarifies priorities and trade-offs Why it matters: When leadership rows in the same direction, AI becomes a force multiplier—not a source of friction. ‍ 10. You Don't Know What You Don't Know ‍ Even the most sophisticated companies don't have full visibility into what's possible with AI across industries. An AI firm brings: • Cross-industry experience with proven use cases • Access to emerging models and methods • A deeper understanding of success patterns—and failure risks Stat to consider: 60% of C-suite leaders say they feel underinformed about what AI is capable of today (Deloitte, 2024). ‍ 11. Financial Modeling That Proves the ROI ‍ AI isn't about hype. It's about quantifiable business value—and a top firm brings the models to prove it. Expect: • P&L impact forecasts for every initiative • Cost-benefit analyses on integration efforts • Gross profit improvement modeling • OPEX reduction tracking • EBITDA contribution by function When you see the numbers, the decision becomes clear. ‍ 12. Function-by-Function Transformation ‍ AI can impact every major business unit—if you know where to look. Cadre AI maps transformation opportunities across: • Finance: Forecasting, reconciliation, anomaly detection • Sales & Marketing: Lead scoring, personalization, ad optimization • Customer Service: AI agents, ticket summarization, intelligent routing • Operations: Demand planning, supply chain optimization, scheduling • HR & People Ops: Talent matching, onboarding, engagement analytics Each function has hidden inefficiencies that AI can unlock. ‍ 13. It Makes You a More Scalable Company ‍ At its core, AI enables doing more with less—without compromising on quality or growth. Scalability outcomes include: • Increased output per employee • Lower unit economics in service delivery • Faster decision cycles via real-time insights • 24/7 operations via automation and intelligent agents The endgame? A more resilient, adaptive, and profitable business. ‍ Final Thoughts: Don't Just Adopt AI. Win With It. ‍ Companies are rushing into AI—but most are doing it reactively and in siloes. If you aren't seeing faster revenue growth or margin expansion through AI you are likely already behind.
