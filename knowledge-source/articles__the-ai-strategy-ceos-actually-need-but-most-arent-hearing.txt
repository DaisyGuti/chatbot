Riley Stricklin Founder | Chief Strategy Officer Every CEO right now is asking the same question: "Where does AI actually fit into my business?" Unfortunately, most of the answers they're getting start with tools and trends. The pitch usually involves a chatbot, an LLM integration, or a flashy productivity hack—none of which tie back to core business value. The result? Confusion, initiative fatigue, and AI projects that fail to make it out of the pilot phase. Here's the truth: real AI strategy doesn't start with ChatGPT—it starts with your business model. ‍ The Common Flaws in "AI Strategy" Today ‍ AI is a top boardroom priority. But most strategies we see fall into the same traps: Tech-first thinking - Teams jump straight into vendor demos without defining the business outcome they're solving for. Unclear business cases - AI pilots launch without ROI models or performance baselines, so success is impossible to measure. Generic use cases - Off-the-shelf chatbots or automation tools that don't map to your unique workflows or value drivers. Lack of cross-functional ownership - AI becomes an "IT initiative" instead of a business initiative—so it stalls. 📉 According to McKinsey, while 83% of executives say AI is a top priority, only 19% have successfully scaled AI across the business. ​ ‍ Cadre's Framework: Flip the Script ‍ At Cadre, we take a different approach: business-first AI strategy. Our framework puts your organization's pain points, processes, and profit levers at the center— not tools . Here's how it works: 1. Business First - Start with a strategic audit of where your margin is thin, workflows are manual, or teams are stretched. Don't guess—map it. 2. Process Mapping - We identify the bottlenecks, redundancies, and friction points inside your core business functions (Sales, Ops, Finance, etc.). 3. Tool Fit - Only once we know the job to be done do we identify which AI tools—LLMs, automations, copilots—fit best and integrate cleanly with your stack. 4. Fast Iteration - We build fast, functional prototypes. We test, measure, and adapt—not with big-bang rollouts, but agile sprints that reduce risk and unlock value early. This isn't theoretical. It's been tested with dozens of mid-market CEOs who needed clarity—and got real operational wins. ‍ Where This Leaves You ‍ If you're a CEO trying to make sense of AI, here's the takeaway: • Don't start with tools. Start with your business. • Don't settle for pilots. Look for process wins that compound. • Don't wait for clarity. Create it with a structured audit. ‍ Ready for Clarity?
